Five rules that do not bend

  1. One colour means “press me”.Teal is for actions only. Information never uses it, so a learner always knows what is tappable.
  2. The highlighter marks one thing.Yellow underlines the quick answer and quoted evidence. Nowhere else, so it keeps its meaning.
  3. Every signal is also a word.Safety, notes, observation versus explanation and claim status each carry a label and a shape (solid, dashed, double rule), never colour alone.
  4. Text first, always readable.Atkinson Hyperlegible for reading, set at 17px with a 65-character measure. The quick answer arrives before anything heavy loads.
  5. Grown-up, not childish.Bricolage Grotesque gives headings warmth without cartoon. No mascots, no confetti, no streaks: progress is shown, not rewarded.

Magnifying glass · explore

Why a magnifying glass makes things look bigger

Quick answer

The lens makes a larger, upright image that fills more of your eye.

Explanation · 3 of 8

Glass bends light

Light changes direction when it passes from air into glass.1 A curved lens bends rays so they seem to come from a larger object.2

refraction→focal length→angular magnification

Based on limited evidence: the larger-image claim rests on one textbook so far.

Explanation · 4 of 8

The lens and your eye work together

  1. The lens bends light from the word.
  2. The rays reach your eye as if from a bigger word.
  3. The image takes up a wider angle, so it looks larger.
