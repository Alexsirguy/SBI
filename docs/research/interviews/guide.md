# Interview guide

About 45 minutes for adults and families, 30 for children. Timings are a guide. Follow the participant's curiosity rather than the order of the script.

**Child-specific** and **Parent-specific** prompts are marked; use them in the child and family segments. Teens get the adult prompts in plainer language.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)

- Introductions, consent check, reminder that they can stop any time.
- "What's something you've wondered about recently: how something works, or why it happens?"
  - **Child:** "Have you ever looked at something and thought 'how does that even work?'"

## 2. Curiosity moments (10 min)

- "Tell me about the last time that happened. Where were you? What did you do next?"
- "What usually stops you finding out?" (time, no one to ask, answers too hard, forgot)
- "When you do find an answer, how do you know it's a good one?"
- **Parent:** "When your child asks 'why', what do you do? How does that feel?"

Listen for: what triggers curiosity; where people look for answers and why; when an answer feels too hard or too simple; trust signals.

## 3. Current tools (5 min)

- "Show me how you'd look up how a bicycle's gears work, right now, on your phone."
- "What's good about that? What's annoying?"
- "Have you used an AI chatbot for questions like this? What happened?"

## 4. The idea (5 min)

Explain, without showing anything: "Imagine you take a photo of something, the app works out what it is, and then explains the science behind it."

- "What would you photograph first?"
- "What would make you trust what it says?"
- "Is there anything you'd _not_ want to photograph or send?"
- **Parent:** "What would you need to know before letting your child use it alone?"

Listen for privacy worries, trust signals such as sources and expert review, and safety expectations.

## 5. Prototype tasks (15 min)

Open the prototype in facilitator mode with the participant's code. Use think-aloud: "Tell me what you're thinking as you go." Don't help unless they're stuck for more than 30 seconds, and note where they got stuck.

1. **Capture and confirm (E1-3):** "Take a photo of something nearby, or pick a sample." Then:
   - confident path (bicycle)
   - ambiguous path (glass): do they notice the alternatives? Do they correct it?
   - unsupported path (medicine): how do they feel about the redirect?
2. **Lesson (E1-4):** pick a question.
   - Answer the one-question check _before_ the lesson, then read for as long as they like.
   - Answer the same check after.
   - Ask: "Was the short answer enough? Did you open more? Was anything too hard?"
   - Try the depth control.
3. **Visuals (E1-5):** show the gear diagram, the 3D model and the simulation, in the order the facilitator panel assigns. After each, ask its comprehension question. Then: "Which helped you understand most? Why?"
4. **Explain back:** "In your own words, how does the thing you picked work?" Note the explanation verbatim. This is the Phase 0 gate measure.

## 6. Wrap-up (5 min)

- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- "Would you use this again? When?"
- **Parent:** "Would you let your child use this? What would you need first?"
- Thank them and give the incentive.
